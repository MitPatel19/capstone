Thunder Bay Context

Why this problem matters here

This project is not trying to copy big-city ride hailing. It is solving a local coordination problem for students and communities in Thunder Bay, where planning, trust, and affordability matter as much as convenience.

Thunder Bay User Stories

Three local stories this platform is designed for

01

International student after evening classes

  • Lives off campus and cannot always rely on one direct bus trip late in the day
  • Needs an affordable, trusted ride with clear price confirmation
  • Values verified drivers and visible ride status more than a random group chat message
02

Healthcare or placement student with early shifts

  • Has placement or work starting before regular schedules feel comfortable
  • Needs a planned ride the night before, not a last-minute scramble in the morning
  • Benefits from OTP pickup and a consistent coordination flow
03

Commuter driver with empty seats

  • Already drives between student housing, campus, downtown, or work zones
  • Wants to share seats safely and recover some cost without becoming a taxi service
  • Needs rider visibility, route clarity, and platform trust before accepting strangers
Solution Overview

What the platform actually delivers

01

Rider-side ride creation

Riders can create requests with pickup, timing, and multiple drop-off points instead of relying on ad hoc chats.

02

Driver approval and activation

License, ID, and insurance upload are built into signup, with admin approval before drivers enter the live ride market.

03

Structured bargaining

The ride only moves forward when both rider and driver explicitly confirm the final negotiated fare.

04

Join-ride sharing logic

Additional riders can request to join, creating rider credits and driver bonuses in a controlled workflow.

05

OTP pickup and live notifications

Pickup verification and real-time updates make the experience safer and more dependable.

06

Admin billing and governance

Billing settings, city tax rules, reports, and user controls make the system operationally useful.

5

Declare payment, check billing, and leave feedback or submit reports if needed.

Technical Architecture

React, TypeScript, Vite, and Tailwind CSS power rider, driver, and admin workflows.

Competitive Positioning

Where we fit against current alternatives

WhatsApp or Facebook groups

  • Fast for informal coordination inside existing communities
  • No driver verification, no structured bargaining, no OTP, no admin oversight
  • Flexible, but unsafe and hard to scale

Uber and Lyft

  • Strong for private on-demand and scheduled rides with polished consumer apps
  • Not designed for community ride coordination, shared student workflows, or admin governance
  • Excellent convenience model, but a different product problem

Uride

  • Relevant because it is a Thunder Bay founded rideshare company and now operates in multiple cities
  • Closer to our local context than Uber or Lyft, but still primarily a rideshare service, not a campus-community coordination platform
  • Shows local market validation, while our model focuses more on trust workflows and shared coordination

Poparide and intercity carpooling

  • Works well for planned cost-sharing trips where drivers already have available seats
  • Less focused on short local rides, institution-linked trust, or admin-managed ecosystems
  • Closer in spirit, but aimed at a different trip pattern

Our platform

  • Best for local community and campus mobility in a trust-sensitive environment
  • Verified onboarding, join rides, OTP pickup, admin workflows, and configurable billing
  • Purpose-built for Thunder Bay style coordination, not generic ride hailing
